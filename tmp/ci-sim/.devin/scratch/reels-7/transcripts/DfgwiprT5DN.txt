If the whole world's internet went down tomorrow, what could your computer still do? Because there's a really interesting project on GitHub that looks like it was made for exactly that day. It's called Project Nomad, and it brings together artificial intelligence, books, maps, and tools you can use without internet. There's just one catch. You have to install and download everything before you go offline. After that, you chat with an AI running on your own computer, and you can drop in your documents for it to search without sending anything to the cloud. Now, imagine pairing that with an entire library. All of Wikipedia, medical references, books, and even survival guides stored inside the same machine. Need to look something up? It's all right there. You can download the map of your region and even take Khan Academy courses with your progress saved even with no internet. And there's also a notepad, cyberchef to analyze data, and a little store to install other tools. In other words, this thing is really complete, almost a post-apocalyptic survival kit. There's even a test that measures how strong your PC is. That makes all the difference, because running an AI asks way more of your machine than just opening a book. Honestly, it looks like someone built a computer for the apocalypse, and I'm sure they did it inside a bunker. But spend just a few days somewhere with no internet signal, and this idea starts to make total sense. And the best part? Project Nomad is free and open-source, and it's already past 42,000 stars on GitHub. For the repo link, comment Nomad.
